# Answer Key and Misconception Guide — Penguin PCA

## Expected reasoning

There is no single correct species ranking or visual story. Accept answers that:

- identify PC1, PC2, or PC3 and cite the direction and approximate coordinate;
- compare two observations using the same coordinate system;
- connect a component to a pattern in the original variables without treating it as one original variable;
- name a limitation such as sample selection, sign orientation, dimensionality reduction, or lack of causal evidence.

## Useful technical reference

For standardized penguin measurements, PC1 commonly reflects a broad body-size direction because bill length, flipper length, and body mass contribute to the same multivariate pattern. PC2 often emphasizes bill-shape contrast. PC3 can preserve a different contrast that is easy to miss in a PC1/PC2 view. Learners should inspect the actual loadings rather than memorize these labels.

The scene coordinates are normalized for display. They are evidence of relative position in this lesson, not the original millimeter or gram units.

## Concrete comparison

The saved scene includes:

- **Adelie 4:** approximately `(-0.318, -0.055, -0.115)`
- **Chinstrap 13:** approximately `(-0.326, -0.019, 0.482)`

These two points are close on PC1 and PC2 but separated on PC3. A strong response predicts that a two-dimensional reading could miss this difference, then uses PC3 as evidence. A strong limitation says that the coordinate does not by itself identify a biological cause or justify a species claim about a new penguin.

## Acceptable exit-ticket response

> Adelie 4 and Chinstrap 13 are close on PC1 and PC2, but Chinstrap 13 is much more positive on PC3. This means the third PCA direction separates them in this selected multivariate representation. It does not prove that PC3 is a species detector or that one penguin is biologically better than another.

Equivalent wording is acceptable when the coordinate evidence and limitation are present.
